03 — Process

Five phases, four gates, and no way to skip forward quietly.

The phases matter less than the gates between them. Each gate has a specific artefact that has to exist before the next phase starts. The process is a ratchet, not a waterfall: you can go back, but you cannot skip ahead.

Intake Five phases Rituals Anti-patterns

/ Before phase one

Work reaches design three ways — roadmap commitments, customer escalations, and things we start ourselves. All three land in the same place and get the same treatment.

Nothing goes straight to Figma. A request that arrives as a solution — "add a filter here" — gets converted back into a problem before anyone opens a file. That conversation takes ten minutes and saves a cycle.

/ How work moves

Frame, Explore, Decide, Build, Learn

Each phase names what it produces and what has to be true before the next one starts. The gate is the part people skip.

/01

Frame

Establish what decision we are improving and for whom. This is the phase most often skipped and most expensive to skip — a beautifully executed answer to the wrong question costs a full cycle.

  • Name the user, the decision, and its frequency
  • Find out what they do today — including the spreadsheet they actually use
  • Agree one success measure with the PM, in advance
  • Write down the constraint that makes this hard
Produces

A one-page brief. One page — if it needs more, the problem is not framed.

Gate

PM and design lead both sign the success measure. No measure, no exploration.

/02

Explore

Deliberately divergent, deliberately cheap. The goal is to make the decision space visible, not to find the answer on day one. Fidelity stays low on purpose — polish creates commitment before commitment is earned.

  • At least three genuinely different approaches, not three shades of one
  • One of them should be the boring version — often it wins
  • Greyscale until the structure is settled
  • Pull an engineer in early to sanity-check feasibility, not to approve
Produces

Three options taken through critique, with the trade-offs written under each.

Gate

Critiqued by the team. An option nobody argued about was not explored.

/03

Decide

Pick one and pressure-test it properly. This is where the four states get drawn, the edge cases get named, and the design stops being a picture and becomes a specification.

  • Empty, loading, error and overflow states, all of them
  • Responsive behaviour and keyboard path
  • Test against the Craft Bar before review, not during
  • Record what was rejected and why — future-you will ask
Produces

A decision record: the choice, the alternatives, the reasoning, the trade-off accepted.

Gate

Design review passed against the Craft Bar. Engineering has read the spec and has no blocking questions.

/04

Build

Design does not end at handoff, because handoff is not a thing that works. The designer stays attached to the build and answers questions in hours, not at the next ceremony.

  • Designer available daily to whoever is implementing
  • Implementation review on staging, against real data, before merge
  • Changes made during build get reflected back into the file
  • Ship behind a flag where the change is significant
Produces

Shipped software plus an updated source file that matches what shipped.

Gate

Designer has seen it running with production-shaped data and signed off.

/05

Learn

The phase everyone drops when the next thing is urgent. We protect it because without it the whole process is just opinion delivered on schedule.

  • Check the success measure agreed in Frame — the actual one
  • Watch three real users, minimum, on the real thing
  • Write what we got wrong, publicly, in one paragraph
  • Feed anything general back into the system or this handbook
Produces

A short written learning, shared in the team channel. One paragraph is enough; zero is not.

Gate

None — this phase closes the loop rather than opening one.

/ The shape of it

A ratchet, not a waterfall. You can go back. You cannot skip forward quietly.

Four gates — Frame · Explore · Decide · Build

/ The week

Two design syncs a week, and nothing else standing

Two fixed points. Everything between them is the work — if a conversation can wait for the next sync, it waits.

/01

Critique

Work in progress only. Finished work is a demo, not a critique. The presenter opens with the decision being improved and the feedback they want — not a tour of the file.

Cite principles by number. Separate "this breaks a rule" from "I would have done it differently" — only the first is binding.

/02

Demo

What actually shipped, shown running against real data, open to the whole company.

It doubles as the week's kickoff: what each person is framing, exploring or deciding next, and where they are stuck.

/ Confession

Anti-patterns we have actually committed

Not hypotheticals. Each of these cost us a cycle, and each one is the reason a gate exists.

/01

Solutioning in intake A stakeholder describes a UI, we build it, and nobody ever names the underlying decision.

/02

Polishing during Explore High fidelity too early makes the first idea unkillable.

/03

The silent gate Moving to Build without a written decision record, then relitigating the same choice three weeks later.

/04

Handoff as a wall Posting a link and disappearing. The gap between the file and the build is filled by whoever is nearest, usually badly.

/05

Skipping Learn Shipping and immediately starting the next thing, so we never find out whether any of it worked.

The process says when a thing can move. The Craft Bar says whether it is any good.
