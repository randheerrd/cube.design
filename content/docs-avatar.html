Components

Avatar

New Last updated Nov 2026

An avatar is a small picture that stands for a person, brand, platform or workspace: a photo or logo, initials, or a placeholder. Circles are for people and rounded squares for brands and platforms; Avatar/group stacks several to show who is involved. The Specifications tab lists every value.

Guidelines Specifications Code (not in Storybook yet) Accessibility (coming soon)

At a glance

A small picture that represents a person, brand, platform or workspace — a photo or logo, initials, or a placeholder. There are two components: Avatar/single (one avatar, Size 12–36 × Roundness) and Avatar/group (a stack of 6 that shows who is involved). What an avatar shows comes from the nested _Avatar-base.

Use it for

Showing who or what something belongs to — a person, brand, platform or workspace — with its name next to it.

Shape

Circle (Roundness = Yes) for people. Rounded square (Roundness = No) for brands, platforms and workspaces.

Content

The image first; if there isn’t one, initials from the name, then the Placeholder.

Anatomy

Avatar/single is a clipped frame — a rounded square or a circle — holding _Avatar-base, which shows an image, initials or a placeholder icon. Avatar/group is several singles overlapping, each with a white ring.

Size

Seven sizes, 36 to 12, shared by Avatar/single and Avatar/group. Pick the size from the text next to it: 20 with 14px text, 24 in chips, 32 in list rows, 36 in headers. Square corners step down with size — radius 6 at 36–32, 4 at 28–20, 2 at 16–12.

Common layouts

Use circles for people and rounded squares for brands, platforms and workspaces — so users can tell them apart at a glance. Pair the avatar with a name whenever space allows: the avatar helps recognition, the name gives certainty.

Do’s and don’ts

Five pairs from the Figma usage guidelines: which shape, size and content to use, and the common mistakes.

New brand

Size

Do

RS
Riya Sharmariya@gobblecube.ai

Match the avatar size to the text next to it (20 with 14px text, 32 in list rows). The alignment stays balanced.

Don’t

RSRiya

Don’t pair a large avatar with small text. It looks unbalanced and wastes space.

Groups

Do

Keep groups short (4–5) with a “+N”. Readable and compact.

Don’t

Don’t use groups at 12–16px. The −6 overlap hides most of each avatar.

Writing content

  • Initials — the first letters of the first and last name, uppercase, at most 2: “Riya Sharma” → RS. ⚠ In Figma the initials are outlined vectors, not editable text — ask for an editable version.
  • Names — put the person’s or brand’s name next to the avatar whenever space allows. Initials alone don’t identify anyone; many people share them.
  • Alt text — an image avatar’s alt text is the person or brand name. When the name is already next to it, the avatar is decorative: alt="".
  • Groups — write the count in text (“Shared with 6 people”) and label the stack for screen readers: aria-label="6 people: …".
  • Hover — a tooltip with the full name, especially in groups.

How to add one in Figma

Add an avatar in 4 steps.

  1. Insert Avatar/single
    Assets → “Avatar/single” (or Avatar/group for several people).
  2. Pick Roundness
    Yes (circle) for people · No (square) for brands, platforms, workspaces.
  3. Pick Size
    Match the text: 20 with 14px text, 24 in chips, 32 in list rows, 36 in headers.
  4. Set the content
    Select the inner _Avatar-base → Property 1: image (then replace the image fill), initials (⚠ the letters are vectors — ask for an editable version), or Placeholder.

Before you hand off

  • Circle for people, square for brands / platforms.
  • Size matches the text next to it.
  • Name shown next to the avatar where space allows.
  • Placeholder used when there’s no image.
  • Groups ≤ 5 with “+N”; not at 12–16px.
